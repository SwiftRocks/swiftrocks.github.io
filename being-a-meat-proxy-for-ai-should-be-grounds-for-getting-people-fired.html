"Reckless" vibe-coding is on the rise in tech companies and should be stopped

Published on 10 Nov 2026

I and many of my colleagues have been observing a massive rise in something that doesn't have an agreed-upon name in the industry as of writing. Some call it "meat proxying", some call it "AI psychosis", some call it "cognitive surrender", and the list goes on. In this post I'll call it "reckless vibe-coding" to make the problem easier to understand.

This is when a developer is assigned a task, be it to review a PR, work on a reasonably complex coding task, or even just asked a simple question, and all they do is prompt an agent "do X" and copy-paste the results back to you without even looking at them, with zero guidance / validation / testing, and oftentimes either failing to disclose or even actively attempting to hide the fact that the work was fully AI generated. Despite being technically prompted by the human, in practice the work was 100% handled by the agent and the presence of the human made no difference whatsoever.

If the task in question is to open a PR, the PR will also as a result have fully AI generated titles/descriptions that may or may not make any sense, and any further commits or review responses will also be fully AI generated. Similarly, if the task is to review a PR, the person will just ask an agent to do it and post comments on the human's behalf. The human never sees the code in question and won't even validate the comments before they are posted. Everything was done by the AI.

Regardless of the case, the pattern is the same: Despite the tasks and actions being in theory "signed" off by the human, in practice the work was handled entirely by AI with zero involvement of the human that prompted it besides the initial "do X" prompt. We've even been observing cases where the human attempted to use this to their advantage to strip themselves of any responsibility regarding the code they wrote and merged (e.g. "This code broke master and took down production? It was opened and merged under my user? No, you see, it's not my problem because it was the AI who did this, not me!").

This behavior is becoming increasingly prevalent in the software engineering industry should be stopped. It should be considered completely unacceptable from an engineering culture point of view as it's no different from other grave engineering mistakes such as pushing straight to master and other types of reckless actions in production and/or involving company property. It should be treated with the same severity as these actions and punished equally. Code reviews are no longer an effective measure against this type of practice because many developers nowadays are also fully delegating the reviews themselves to AI, meaning AI code can (and will) easily get generated and land into master without a single human even being made aware of it, because every "human" you may spot having conversations and generating reviews may turn out to actually be an AI pretending to be the human in question.

Reckless vibe-coding is particularly worse than everything we’ve seen so far because the devs who do this are effectively:

  • Wasting your time, because if all they do is proxy their work to an agent, you might as well skip the person and do that yourself
  • Forcing you to do their work for them, because you now have to not only review and validate agentic output that they did not, but also potentially have to be one dealing with the consequences of any nonsense code that they may have managed to merge when you were not paying attention
  • Wasting the company's time and resources, as this behavior is basically a convoluted way of stating that they are 1) getting paid a salary to do something anyone could have done, 2) harming the company's products (if the work involves pushing or reviewing PRs; as vibe-coded PRs are almost always complete nonsense), and 3) wasting the time of anyone having to deal with them, particularly when the dev intentionally tries to hide their actions and pass AI generated output as their own words or code

It's essentially a novel way to silently scam both the company and the colleagues and is in my eyes no different even from other software engineering scams such as covertly having two jobs or hiring a third-party to do your work for you and act as if they were you while you do something else.

I use the word “scam” intentionally here because it really feels that some people have weaponized AI to trick their managers into believing that they are being productive by opening hundreds of PRs with very complex descriptions and bold claims of having fixed major issues and/or greatly improved systems, when in reality a deeper look reveals that this "work" is actually just a bunch of unreviewed vibe-coded nonsense that not only doesn't fix or improves anything at all, but also actively harms whatever products they are "working" with and massively complicates the work of the devs who have to deal with the consequences of said reckless code and actually care about building good products and using AI responsibly.

This is getting so insane that I've even had cases where I would DM someone on Slack and notice right away that I'm talking to an AI. Not in the sense that they've set up an agent to handle their messages for them (wouldn't be surprised if people start doing this though), but that they are copying my question to an agent and pasting back the response (without disclosure, so making it seem that they wrote it themselves). WTF?

Just because AI has made it very easy to generate code, it doesn’t mean that the generated code is guaranteed to be correct and safe. Even with “insert latest frontier model name here”, generating good code and code reviews still requires substantial human guidance and cannot be fully delegated to AI. Just like we as an industry have developed strict standards on what is and isn’t acceptable in the “old way” of writing code, we must now do the same in this new environment, starting with forbidding this form of reckless usage of AI for anything that is not prototyping.

(Disclaimer: This post was originally called “Being a meat proxy for AI should be grounds for getting people fired”. I’ve rewritten it to make the tone more neutral, but the content and message it aims to communicate is largely the same.)
